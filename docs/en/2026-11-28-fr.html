Euro area: household-perceived inflation soars in October 2026

The results of the ECB survey reveal a marked increase in inflation perceived by consumers, reaching 4.1%. This sentiment contrasts with stagnant income growth, weighing on household morale.

The ECB's Consumer Expectations Survey for October 2026 shows that the inflation rate perceived over the last 12 months has climbed to 4.1%, up from 3.6% in September. This is the highest level since July 2024. At the same time, one-year inflation expectations also rose, from 3.1% to 3.5%. This rise in expectations is accompanied by growing uncertainty among respondents. Lower-income households are particularly affected, reporting higher perceived inflation than wealthier households. This climate is exacerbated by a decline in nominal income growth expectations.

Analysis

The rise in perceived inflation, although higher than official inflation, directly influences household consumption behavior. When consumers anticipate a price increase, they may reduce their spending or demand wage increases, which fuels an inflationary spiral. The decline in nominal income expectations (from 1.1% to 0.6%) suggests a deterioration in real purchasing power, despite a perception of stability in the labor market. This gap between the perceived cost of living and disposable income creates significant social and economic pressure. Central banks must navigate with caution to prevent these expectations from becoming durably anchored in the economy.

Critical view

The survey highlights a sharp disparity between incomes, with the poorest quintile bearing the full brunt of inflation without benefiting from the same income growth as the wealthiest. The document does not specify the corrective measures envisaged by the ECB to counter this de-anchoring of expectations. Furthermore, growing uncertainty regarding short-term forecasts makes monetary policy more difficult to calibrate.

Possible solutions

Strengthen educational communication on price stability objectives to stabilize expectations. Implement targeted support mechanisms for households in the first income quintile, who are the most vulnerable to inflation. Encourage greater transparency regarding exogenous factors (energy, geopolitics) influencing prices to avoid inflationary panic.

Key takeaways
  • Inflation perceived by households reaches 4.1%, a peak since 2024.
  • Nominal income growth expectations fall to 0.6%.
  • A sharp disparity remains between lower-income and wealthier households.

Energy tensions and financial risks: the ECB's stern diagnosis

During its October 2026 meeting, the ECB expressed deep concern over soaring energy prices and the risks associated with massive investments in artificial intelligence.

The Governing Council discussions on October 28 and 29, 2026, highlighted a deterioration in the inflation outlook. Oil and gas prices have exceeded the most pessimistic scenarios, driven by tensions in the Middle East. Diesel refining margins are reaching historical highs, surpassing levels observed at the start of the conflict in Ukraine. This situation has led to an upward adjustment of inflation expectations for 2027 and 2028. Furthermore, financial markets are now concerned about the viability of investments in AI, which are often debt-financed.

Analysis

The current energy shock acts as an inflation multiplier, particularly in an import-dependent euro area. Rising transport and raw material costs weigh on corporate margins, which risks translating into higher consumer prices. The decoupling observed between energy prices and interest rates shows that the ECB is trying not to react mechanically to every shock, in order to avoid excessive market volatility. However, the persistence of these inflationary pressures beyond 2028 complicates the normalization of monetary policy.

Critical view

The report highlights the risk associated with massive debt for AI, but remains vague on concrete prudential regulation measures. Dependence on geopolitical scenarios makes the ECB's forecasts extremely fragile. The risk of stagflation (low growth and high inflation) is not explicitly ruled out.

Possible solutions

Diversify energy supply sources to reduce vulnerability to Middle East shocks. Closely monitor bank exposure to highly indebted companies in the AI sector. Maintain a restrictive but flexible monetary policy to anchor long-term inflation expectations.

Key takeaways
  • Energy prices are exceeding the most pessimistic scenarios.
  • Inflation expectations remain high through 2028.
  • Growing concern over debt financing for AI projects.

Payments: bank cards overtake cash in the euro area

For the first time, card payments have surpassed cash usage at physical points of sale in the euro area. Despite this digital transition, citizens remain attached to the freedom of choice.

According to the ECB's SPACE study published in 2026, bank cards now account for 45% of in-store transactions, compared to 44% for cash. Mobile payments are also growing, reaching 9% of the total. This development marks a historic turning point since data collection began in 2016. However, consumer preference for cash remains stable: a large majority consider it essential to maintain the option to pay with cash. Access to ATMs remains generally satisfactory, although 19% of users have encountered difficulties.

Analysis

The rise of digital payments reflects the modernization of infrastructure and increased technological adoption by merchants. However, the sustained high demand for cash highlights a desire for privacy and perceived security in the event of digital system failures. In response, the ECB is seeking to guarantee this freedom of choice by working on a central bank digital currency project (the digital euro). This transition poses challenges in terms of financial inclusion, particularly for less tech-savvy or more vulnerable populations.

Critical view

The study notes that 12% of consumers faced a digital payment refusal at least once a month, showing that digital infrastructure is not yet infallible. The source does not detail the transaction costs for small merchants associated with this transition. Access to cash, while considered easy, shows signs of deterioration compared to 2024.

Possible solutions

Ensure the maintenance of a sufficient ATM network to guarantee financial inclusion. Accelerate the development of the digital euro as a secure and sovereign alternative to private solutions. Support merchants in upgrading their payment systems to reduce transaction failures.

Key takeaways
  • Bank cards account for 45% of in-store payments.
  • 82% of consumers consider it important to be able to pay digitally.
  • Freedom of choice between cash and digital remains a priority for 62% of citizens.

Sweden integrates the European T2 payment system

Sweden has reached an agreement with the ECB to join the T2 system, the European infrastructure for large-value payment settlement, thereby strengthening European financial integration.

ECB President Christine Lagarde and Governor of the Swedish Riksbank Erik Thedéen signed an agreement allowing Sweden to use T2 for settlements in Swedish krona. This migration, scheduled for February 2030, marks the transition from a national platform to a shared European infrastructure. T2 is the Eurosystem's real-time gross settlement system, ensuring the security and efficiency of high-value financial transactions. Sweden thus joins Denmark, which already uses this infrastructure for its Danish krone settlements.

Analysis

This integration strengthens Europe's strategic autonomy in payments. By pooling infrastructure, central banks reduce maintenance costs and improve resilience against financial crises. For Swedish financial institutions, this means adopting common European standards, facilitating cross-border trade and enhancing settlement security. It is a key step toward a more unified European financial system capable of operating effectively even during periods of high market stress.

Critical view

The shift to a common infrastructure carries risks of technological dependence on the ECB's centralized system. The implementation timeline (2030) is distant, leaving a period of uncertainty regarding the technological evolution of payment systems in the meantime. No information is provided on transition costs for Swedish commercial banks.

Possible solutions

Harmonize security protocols between national banks and the T2 system to prevent any vulnerabilities during the migration. Encourage other non-euro area countries to join similar infrastructures to increase the efficiency of the single market. Invest in the cybersecurity of shared platforms to counter growing threats.

Key takeaways
  • Sweden joins the European T2 payment system.
  • The migration is scheduled for February 2030.
  • The goal is to strengthen the security and efficiency of payments in Swedish krona.
