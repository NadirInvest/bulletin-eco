Financial stability: the ECB is concerned about AI exposure

The latest ECB financial stability report warns of increased risks linked to stock market valuations of artificial intelligence. Geopolitical tensions and fiscal imbalances are also weighing on the resilience of the euro area.

The ECB highlights persistent geopolitical uncertainty and a growing dependence on AI-related technologies. While the banking sector appears resilient to shocks, non-bank financial intermediaries show increased vulnerabilities. Investments in AI are often debt-financed, creating a risk of contagion from equity markets to bond markets. At the same time, sovereign funding needs are rising, making markets more sensitive to news.

Analysis

Enthusiasm for AI is based on highly ambitious earnings expectations, which exposes investors to a sharp correction if results do not follow. Such volatility could restrict the room for maneuver of governments already weakened by energy costs and low growth. For companies, concentration on a few third-party critical service providers poses a systemic risk in the event of a cyberattack. Households, indirectly exposed through their investments or the stability of their bank, could suffer the repercussions of an abrupt repricing.

Critical view

The report remains vague on the precise scale of the debt hidden behind the financing of AI infrastructure. It also does not detail specific default scenarios for less regulated non-bank actors.

Possible solutions

Strengthen macroprudential safeguard mechanisms for non-bank institutions. Increase the diversification of critical technology service providers to limit concentration risk.

The Fed adjusts its payment service fees for 2027

The US Federal Reserve has announced a revision of its payment service fees, effective January 1, 2027. This update is accompanied by an optimization of check processing services in response to their declining usage.

The law requires the Fed to recover the costs of its payment services over the long term. The adjustments planned for 2027 aim to achieve a recovery rate of 101.5% of actual and imputed expenses. Check volume continues to decline, forcing the institution to adapt its operational infrastructure. Despite this digital trend, the check remains an essential payment instrument for many actors.

Analysis

An average increase of 1.4% on mature services will be applied, including an increase in check processing fees. These operational changes aim to streamline costs while maintaining the fluidity of the payment system. For banks and credit unions, this cost increase will need to be managed within their fee structures. The optimization of processing windows during low-volume periods confirms the Fed's desire to modernize its operations.

Critical view

The source does not specify what impact this fee increase might have on the final costs borne by retail users. The shift to digital is inevitable but raises the question of the inclusion of populations dependent on checks.

Possible solutions

Encourage the transition to more efficient instant payment systems. Periodically review operational processing models to minimize fixed costs during periods of declining volumes.

The scaling imperative for the European industry

Europe has solid fundamentals in terms of quality of life but suffers from a lack of critical mass in the face of global competition. Isabel Schnabel advocates for the optimal exploitation of the Single Market to transform innovation into economic growth.

In a context of technological transformations and geopolitical tensions, Europe is often perceived as a continent in decline. This view is considered misleading, as it obscures European successes in terms of institutions and social protection. The major challenge identified is not a lack of ideas or talent, but the inability to convert these assets into a competitive industrial scale. Market fragmentation is currently hindering technological deployment.

Analysis

The objective is to move from a logic of 27 national markets to a truly unified market to create a strong European brand. This integration would allow companies to benefit from economies of scale that are essential for competing with international powers. For households, this strengthening could translate into better competitiveness and the preservation of living standards. Ultimately, this strategy aims to solidify the European economic foundations in the face of exogenous shocks.

Critical view

The analysis does not offer a concrete solution for removing the regulatory hurdles often cited by companies as barriers to investment. Tax and administrative disparities between Member States are only superficially addressed.

Possible solutions

Finalize and deepen the integration of the Single Market to facilitate cross-border access for companies. Foster the emergence of European champions capable of competing on a global scale.
