Financial stability: the ECB warns of risks linked to AI and sovereign debt

In its November 2026 report, the European Central Bank highlights increased fragility in the financial system. Massive investments in artificial intelligence and the burden of public debt constitute the two major threats to the stability of the eurozone.

The ECB has published its semi-annual report on financial stability, identifying risks that have intensified over the past year. Enthusiasm for AI has triggered a rise in stock market valuations, which are now disconnected from actual profit expectations. At the same time, the public debts of member states are weighing heavily on bond markets. Geopolitical tensions and persistent energy costs are further complicating the fiscal situation for governments. While resilient, the financial system shows weaknesses in the non-bank sector, which is less regulated than traditional banks. This interconnection between banks and non-bank actors could amplify a crisis in the event of a sudden shock.

Analysis

The risk mechanism is based on a potential sharp market correction: if AI expectations are disappointed, losses could spread from equities to bonds. The concentration of technology services among a few third-party providers also creates systemic vulnerability to cyberattacks. For companies, this means potentially more expensive and volatile access to financing. Households, for their part, could suffer the consequences of increased fragility if banks tighten their credit conditions to offset these risks. The current resilience of the system is supported by strong capital buffers, but the fiscal room for maneuver of states is dangerously diminishing.

Critical view

The report highlights the risks but remains evasive on concrete tools to counter a liquidity crisis in the non-bank sector. It also does not specify how states could concretely reduce their debt without curbing already weak growth. Technological dependence is identified, but no immediate digital sovereignty solution is proposed.

Possible solutions

Strengthen macroprudential safeguards for non-bank institutions to limit contagion. Diversify funding sources to reduce dependence on a few technology giants. Accelerate the reduction of public deficits to restore fiscal credibility to member states.

Key takeaways
  • Excessive stock market valuations linked to AI.
  • Increased fragility of sovereign debts.
  • Risk of contagion between banks and the non-bank sector.

The ECB simplifies the supervision of small European banks

To support growth and competitiveness, the ECB proposes to ease regulatory constraints on small banking institutions. The objective is to adapt supervision to the simplicity of their business models.

In Europe, small banks play a crucial role in financing local businesses and households, particularly in Germany with the 'Mittelstand'. Currently, 75% of banks supervised by the ECB are classified as 'small and non-complex'. These institutions are sometimes subject to rules designed for international banking giants. The ECB acknowledges that this uniform approach creates unnecessary complexity. A recent review identified ways to reduce the administrative burden without compromising the security of depositors.

Analysis

Banking regulation is based on the principle of proportionality: the riskier and more complex a bank is, the more it is monitored. By simplifying reporting requirements (already reduced by 70% for these entities), the ECB aims to free up operational resources for local banks. This allows institutions to focus on their core business: lending to the real economy. Ultimately, this could promote better credit distribution and strengthen the resilience of the regional economic fabric. However, this simplification does not mean a lowering of customer protection standards.

Critical view

The main risk is creating a blind spot in oversight if the 'simplicity' criteria are poorly assessed. The source does not detail the safeguards that will prevent these small banks from taking excessive risks under the guise of simplification. The actual effectiveness of these measures will depend on implementation by national authorities.

Possible solutions

Finalize simplification proposals to reduce compliance costs. Maintain targeted oversight on real risks rather than data volume. Encourage the sharing of best practices among small banks to strengthen their robustness.

Key takeaways
  • 75% of banks in the eurozone are considered 'small and non-complex'.
  • Reducing reporting requirements to foster efficiency.
  • Maintaining security standards despite simplification.

The Federal Reserve adjusts its fees and modernizes its payment services.

The Fed announces a fee increase for payment services effective in 2027. This decision aims to cover operational costs while modernizing check processing, the use of which is declining.

The law requires the Federal Reserve to recover the costs of its payment services, including a return on equity equivalent to that of the private sector. For 2027, the Fed projects an average price increase of 1.4% for its mature services. At the same time, the volume of checks processed by the Fed is decreasing, forcing the institution to optimize its processes. These adjustments apply exclusively to banks and credit unions, not to end users.

Analysis

This pricing mechanism ensures that the centralized payment system is not subsidized by taxpayers. The fee increase reflects a commitment to operational efficiency in a context where digital services are overtaking traditional instruments. For banks, this increase is an additional structural cost that could be passed on to corporate banking services. The optimization of check processing windows demonstrates the Fed's desire to streamline its aging infrastructure without abandoning services necessary for economic actors.

Critical view

The source does not specify whether this fee increase could discourage the use of certain essential payment services. The impact on small financial institutions, which have less room to absorb these costs, is not addressed. Reliance on Fed payment systems remains a point of operational vulnerability.

Possible solutions

Accelerate the transition to instant payment systems to reduce reliance on checks. Improve cost transparency for user banks. Invest in the cybersecurity of payment infrastructure to anticipate future risks.

Key takeaways
  • Average 1.4% increase in Fed fees in 2027.
  • Cost recovery target set at 101.5%.
  • Optimization of check processing in response to declining volumes.
